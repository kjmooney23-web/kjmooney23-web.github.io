Featured Project

Colorado's Broadband Opportunity

Landscape, Competition & Expansion Strategy

Analysis combining FCC Broadband Data Collection (Dec 2025) and the IBM Telco Customer Churn dataset (Kaggle, 2018) to explore Cable vs. Fiber competition, expansion opportunities, and churn risk strategy across 2.7M locations. Built with SQL & Tableau.

SQL Tableau FCC Data IBM Telco (Kaggle) 2.7M Locations
1

Background & Overview

This project analyzes Colorado's broadband landscape using the FCC Broadband Data Collection (December 2025). The goal was to understand the competitive dynamics between Cable and Fiber providers, identify geographic white space for network expansion, and assess customer churn risk across service tiers. The analysis was built entirely with SQL for data engineering and Tableau for visualization, structured as a 4-dashboard story.

Data Structure Overview

The FCC dataset contains availability records at the location level across Colorado — approximately 2.7 million locations. Key fields include: technology type (Cable, Fiber, DSL, Fixed Wireless), provider name, maximum advertised download/upload speeds, and geographic identifiers (county, census block). Data was cleaned and aggregated in SQL, with derived metrics created for competitive density, speed ratios, and market share calculations. A second dataset — the IBM Telco Customer Churn dataset (Kaggle, 2018) — was joined to enrich the churn risk analysis with customer behavior attributes including contract type, tenure, monthly charges, and service tier.

Executive Summary

Cable providers currently dominate Colorado's broadband market, controlling approximately 59% of serviceable locations. However, Fiber is gaining ground rapidly, with upload speeds averaging 13x faster than Cable. Using both FCC Broadband Data Collection (Dec 2025) and the IBM Telco Customer Churn dataset (Kaggle, 2018), four dashboards were built to tell this story: (1) Market Landscape — provider share and technology mix; (2) Competitive Dynamics — head-to-head speed and coverage comparisons; (3) Expansion Opportunity — geographic white space analysis by county; (4) Churn Risk — contract type and speed tier analysis to predict retention risk.

Insights Deep Dive

Key findings from the analysis: Cable incumbents hold 59% market share but are concentrated in metro Denver and Front Range corridors. Fiber providers show 13x faster upload speeds on average, a compelling differentiator for remote workers. Rural counties — particularly in the San Luis Valley and Eastern Plains — show significant underserved populations with limited provider choice. Month-to-month contract customers in low-speed tiers represent the highest churn risk segment, accounting for a disproportionate share of potential subscriber loss.

Recommendations

Based on the analysis, three strategic recommendations were identified: (1) Expansion Priority — target rural counties with fewer than 2 competing providers and populations above 5,000 for near-term fiber buildout; (2) Speed Tier Positioning — lead with upload speed as a differentiator in marketing, particularly for remote work and small business segments; (3) Retention Strategy — offer 12-month contract incentives to month-to-month customers in Tiers 1–2 speed segments to reduce churn risk before competitor entry.

Caveats & Assumptions

Several limitations should be noted: FCC availability data reflects advertised coverage, not actual subscriptions or experienced speeds. Provider-reported speeds may overstate real-world performance. The dataset does not include pricing information, which would strengthen churn risk modeling. Geographic analysis is at the census block level and may not capture hyperlocal infrastructure gaps. Future iterations of this analysis would benefit from integrating subscription data, pricing tiers, and customer satisfaction scores.
